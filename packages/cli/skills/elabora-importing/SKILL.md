## A module made with Storyline or Rise
When the author brings a Storyline 360 project (`.story`), a course Storyline published (a folder, a zip, its `story.html`) or a Rise 360 export (any year, 2017 to 2026: the course is read from the player page, a locale file or `runtime-data.js`; a Rise course that holds Storyline blocks is imported as the Rise course and each Storyline block is reported), import it rather than rebuilding it by hand: `elabora import <file> <new folder>` (beta; without a folder it goes into the app's library). Then read `import-report.json` in the new course: its `summary`, its `decisions` (tell the author each one and its choices; `--choose <decision>=<choice>` imports again with another, into a new folder), its `losses`, and the `note` of the blocks that stand for a feature Elabora has no block for yet. Finish the work they list: write the plan's objective and the items' kinds (the plan is a draft), alternative texts, captions and transcripts, real feedback where a question got a generic one, media to replace (Articulate's Content Library may not be used outside its tools), and lay screens out again where the reading order is wrong. Check slides with `elabora look` and `elabora fit`. Rise's blocks land on the starter blocks (statements, quotes, galleries, columns, dividers, tabs and flip cards, process and timeline as `steps`, labeled graphics as `hotspots`, sorting, matching and fill in the blank as `sort` and `fill-in`, charts as charts) and Storyline's matching, sequence, fill-in, word bank and Likert questions on `sort`, `fill-in` and `reflection`: write the feedback they lack (`W_ACTIVITY_NO_FEEDBACK`), check markers' places (`W_HOTSPOTS_CLOSE`), shorten long tab labels, and change a form with `elabora_convert_block` rather than by hand (skill `elabora-blocks`). A Rise scenario becomes a dialogue block: reread it with `elabora dialogue show` and `paths`, give its options a quality, and see skill `elabora-dialogues`.
