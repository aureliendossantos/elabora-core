# D72. Blocks placed by hand: the flow first, a place where the author drops it, back in the flow below the minimum window (6 Nov 2026)

Authors coming from Storyline, PowerPoint or Canva expect to put a block where they want it and to size it by hand. Elabora's screens are flows (a column, or the zones of a layout) that hold at every window, on phones too, and are read in one order. The study of 6 Nov (Lin 52, `elabora-notes/reports/placement-libre/rapport.md`) compared four directions; the founder chose the third, « le fil d'abord », with the tidying of the fourth and the zones of the second, and asked for slides and scroll pages in the first version: « Conceptuellement, les slides ou les pages Rise sont la même chose chez nous. C'est juste une grande slide. »

Decided:
- **A block placed by hand keeps its anchor.** `block.place: { x, y, width, height?, z? }` (schemas 0.1.11), only on the blocks of a screen itself (`E_PLACED_NESTED` in a layer or a component's slots). The anchor is the block's slot and its rank in the screen's list, as before: its reading order, its focus order, and where it goes when it is not placed. Removing `place` puts the block back there.
- **Units: shares of the placement frame.** The frame is the reference window (`presentation.fit.reference`, 1600×900 by default) at the width of the screen's area (`#t-main`), at most the reference width in rem (centred beyond it). `x` and `width` are shares of its width, `y` and `height` of its height (the reference ratio at that width): the same unit as `hotspots` and `focus`, readable as px at the reference (`x × 1600`). `height` left out: the block's content sets it (a picture, its proportions); with it, a picture fills the box around its focal point. `z` orders placed blocks (higher in front); placed blocks are drawn over the flow.
- **Slides and pages, one rule for `y`.** In slides the frame is centred in the slide and `y` is measured from its top; a block that would leave a slide shorter than the frame (its bars take room) is kept inside it. In scroll presentation `y` is measured from the block's anchor, the top of the next block of its list in the flow (else the end of the one before it): a placed block moves with the text before it, as « Move with text » does in Word and Pages. A page is a tall slide whose blocks follow their text.
- **Below the minimum, the flow.** At windows narrower than `presentation.fit.minimum` (1280 px by default, slides and pages alike), on phones and portrait tablets, and when the learner enlarges the text (« Affichage »), a placed block reads in the flow at its anchor: the threshold where a slide already reads as a page. The editor's canvas places them at any width but a phone's (page option `placing=editor`); its menu « Fenêtre de 1280 × 720 » shows the minimum.
- **The player places, CSS draws.** `@elabora/player` `placement.ts` measures the frame and each anchor from the page's offsets (what a fade or a glide moves for a moment is left out) and writes px in custom properties of the block's containing block; `player.css` lifts `.t-block[data-t-place]` with them. Every read before every write, again on resize, on a step shown, a size class crossed, fonts loaded. `#elabora-app[data-t-placing]` says it is on; a slot holding only placed blocks has `[data-t-placed-only]` for a layout to fold it.
- **The editor's canvas.** `elabora/lift` shows a dragged block out of the flow at a box of the viewport (it stays there when the page scrolls), or at a place in the frame (a handle's preview); `release: "render"` holds it until the render of the change. A block lifted from the flow leaves a stand-in of its size (`.t-lift-trace`): nothing around it moves while it is dragged, the flow changes once it is let go (the founder, 6 Nov); its box is the layout's `home`, and the flow has a place under it too, both « where it was ». `elabora/motion` makes the next render glide from what the page shows (FLIP, the player's curve; less motion: at once); while blocks glide, the layout gives where they land and `glide { from, ms, elapsed, easing }`, so that the host's marks run the same glide. Its layout report gives `placed`, `anchor`, `covers` (the blocks whose lines of text it lies over) and `placing`: the frame, the flow's places zone by zone (each zone with its slot's `label` and `refuses`, the blocks of the screen it does not accept or has no room left for, by E_COMPONENT_SLOT's rules), the column's widths. The engine's `canvasView { preview }` renders a change before it is made (a handle's step), nothing written.
- **The app's gestures** (direction C): a drag goes to a place in the flow when the dragged block's box comes into a zone, the place nearest to its top, whatever point the pointer holds (the founder's case: a large picture held by its corner, its top near its place, goes back there); anywhere else, or with the key held (⌘ on a Mac, Alt elsewhere, as PowerPoint does), it is placed by hand and its anchor follows: the zone on its side, the rank at its height. Only the zones that take the block offer it a place or an anchor (a scene keeps its object; the founder, 6 Nov), so that a gesture never makes a module invalid. Handles step through the sizes of the zone (the column's half, full, wide and bleed widths), gliding from one to the next; with the key, or on a placed block, any size. The page scrolls under a drag near the stage's edges and with the wheel, the block under the pointer. The first block an author places brings a note, once per author, with « Mettre au propre » and « Ranger ».
- **Commands.** `place_block { block, place | null, slot?, after?, position? }`: the place and the anchor in one change (CLI `elabora block place`); `arrange_screen { screen, blocks? }`, « Ranger »: placed blocks back in the flow, each at its anchor (MCP `elabora_arrange_screen`, CLI `elabora screen arrange`); `update_block` takes `place` (MCP `elabora_update_block`). The summary's outline counts each screen's placed blocks.
- **Checks.** `I_BLOCK_PLACED` (a screen's placed blocks, for the agent), `E_PLACED_NESTED`, `W_PLACED_OUTSIDE` (validate); `W_PLACED_OVERLAP` (lines of text covered), `W_PLACED_OVERFLOW` (content past its height or the slide), `W_PLACED_TARGET` (a button under 44 px), measured by `elabora fit` at the reference and the minimum; pages with placed blocks are measured at the minimum too.
- **« Mettre au propre ».** The author's AI reads the places as a sketch and puts the blocks in a layout of the module, or a new one, then removes the places; the app's request names the other screens with placed blocks, for one visual language.

Weighed:
- **`y` from the top of a page.** Rejected: a paragraph added above, or text reflowing at 1280 px (rem shrinks less than the window), would leave a placed block beside the wrong text; down a long page the gap grows.
- **The frame in rem (100rem wide).** Rejected: at 1280 px a 100rem frame is 1400 px, wider than the window; blocks near its edges would be cut.
- **A position per window size.** Rejected: the complaint best documented in other tools (Captivate, Rise, Wix).
- **The pointer decides between the flow and a place.** Rejected by the founder: a large block held far from its edge ends up placed beside the line it was meant to go back to.
- **Space, then ⌥, for the key.** Space (the study's choice) was replaced by the founder with ⌘/Alt, PowerPoint's.

*Revisit if:* authors ask for placed blocks in layers or inside components; the editor's canvas shows the bars' room (the slide in the editor is taller than in a package); Storyline's import keeps positions as placed blocks (next, after this version); an option keeps a whole screen at scale below the minimum (`presentation.placed: "scale"`, studied).
