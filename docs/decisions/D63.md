# D63. Starter blocks for scroll courses: few blocks with presentations, three primitives, three activities (24 Oct 2026)

Authors used to building courses in Rise 360 expect a legible palette of ready-made blocks. Until now each course wrote its own (`medieval-charts` wrote six). After research on Rise's catalogue, authors' complaints, competitors' palettes, and the learning and accessibility evidence for each block type, the founder approved a proposal and its leanings (24 Oct 2026). The research and the proposal are kept with the project's notes.

Weighed:

- **Copy Rise's catalogue:** 11 families and about 60 fixed variants. Rise itself switches most variants of a family without losing content, because they are presentations of the same data. A flat catalogue grows without telling authors what each block is for; H5P's 54 types show a long tail nobody uses. Rejected.
- **One "reveal" block and one "question" block holding every form.** Their data differ (titled items, ordered items, regions on a picture; options, blanks, pairs), and a schema per type is what agents and the validator read best. Rejected.
- **A block per learner action and data shape, its forms a `presentation` prop.** About ten new blocks and four extended ones cover every Rise variant and what Rise lacks. Chosen.

## Conventions

- **`presentation`.** A block with several forms declares an enum prop `presentation`, its first value the default. Templates branch with `data-if="presentation == tabs"` and expose it as a class on their root (`t-panels t-panels--{{presentation}}`). Switching it never touches the words. The app shows the values as variants in « Ajouter un bloc » and as a segmented choice in the inspector, with its own French labels: schemas carry no English `x-enum-labels`.
- **Items.** Blocks made of items share one shape where they can: `id` (`^[a-z0-9-]{1,24}$`, stable: visited marks, translations and conversions name it), `title`, `markdown`, `image` and `alt`, `date`. This is what makes « Transformer en… » possible between them.
- **Focal point.** `focus: { "x": 0..1, "y": 0..1 }` on an image becomes `object-position`: crops on phones keep the subject.
- **Full bleed.** A block's `width` may be `bleed`: edge to edge of the window in scroll presentation, of the slide in slides. A template asks for it from its root with `data-t-bleed` (an image band does). Text in a bleed block keeps the content width.
- **Optional blocks.** A block may say `optional: true` (a transcript, extra detail). It counts neither for a step that waits for exploration nor for `completeWhen: "interactions-done"`.
- **Less motion.** Every movement has a reduced-motion form (a short fade, or none). Nothing pulses or loops.
- **No layout shift.** Blocks that show one thing at a time (tabs, cards, a carousel, a tour) reserve the room of their tallest item, as activities reserve their tallest feedback.

## Primitives (any template)

Hooks the player handles, documented in [Components](../components.md) §3 and checked like the others.

| Hook | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `data-t-sequence` on a container; `data-t-item="k"` on each item; `data-t-prev`, `data-t-next` (buttons); optional `data-t-goto="k"` (buttons), `data-t-position` | One item at a time. The others are `hidden`. It follows the APG carousel pattern with buttons and never moves by itself. Arrow keys work inside it and a swipe works on touch. It writes `data-t-current` on the container and `aria-current` on the goto button. `data-t-position` receives "2 / 5" from the player's labels. The container reserves the tallest item's room. An item is visited once shown, and showing it fires `click` with its name. |
| `data-t-card="k"` holding `data-t-face="front"` and `data-t-face="back"`; `data-t-flip="k"` (button) | A flip card. `data-t-flipped` goes on the card, and the hidden face is `inert` and `aria-hidden`. The button's label comes from the player's labels (TPGi's pattern). The card is visited once flipped. Under reduced motion the flip is a cross-fade. |
| `data-t-zoom` (a button holding an `<img>`) | Opens the image at full size in the player's lightbox, a modal dialog with the image's alt and an optional `data-t-caption`. Escape closes it and focus returns. Several zoom buttons in one block give previous and next. |
| `data-t-exclusive` on a container of `data-t-toggle` buttons | Opening one part closes the others. Escape closes the open part and returns focus to its toggle. `data-t-exclusive="popover"` also closes it on a click outside. |
| `<details data-t-details="k">` | A native disclosure that counts as visited once opened. |
| `data-t-reveal="k"` on an element | Visited once it has been in view (a timeline's event, a scrolling step). |

`item`, `flip`, `details` and `reveal` are controls (docs/components.md, Controls): visited, saved, read by `{ "visited": … }` conditions and by steps that wait for exploration.

## Steps that wait for exploration

`steps.<n>.opens: "explored"`: the Continue of step *n* is shown but locked until every activity of the steps above is answered and every control of their blocks is visited, `optional` blocks aside. Locked, it says what is left ("2 cards left", from the player's labels). This is Rise's Continue with "complete block above", with a reason given. In slides, Next is locked the same way.

**Notes.**
- **`statement`.** With an `author` it is a quote in every presentation: `<figure>`, `<blockquote>`, `<figcaption>`. The `image` presentation is a bleed block over the theme's scrim, and `elabora fit` measures its contrast against the pixels behind the text.
- **`divider`.** Numbers come from a CSS counter on the screen (`decimal-leading-zero`). They are decorative: dividers are hidden from screen readers, and a line is an `<hr>`.
- **`accordion`.**
  - It keeps its id, so no format migration is needed. The accordion uses `data-t-details`.
  - Tabs become an accordion in containers narrower than 560 px.
  - Cards follow the flip primitive. Their front is the title and the image; their back is the text.
- **`steps`.**
  - The carousel's intro and summary are items of the sequence (`intro`, `summary`), so "every item visited" means the end was reached.
  - The timeline's events and the scrolling steps are `data-t-reveal`.
  - The scroll presentation keeps native scroll: no scrolljacking. A step is current while it crosses the middle of the window. Without JavaScript or under reduced motion, each step's image shows with its step. `build` stacks the images as layers; `focus` moves over regions of `figureImage`.
- **`hotspots`.** Markers are at least 44 px, numbered by default, and never pulse. In containers narrower than 560 px the panels become a numbered list under the image, as Adapt does. `tour` walks the markers with the sequence primitive.
- **`quiz` `image`.** The regions are the options. Screen readers hear neutral names ("Zone 1", from the labels) until the answer is final, so the picture does not give it away.
- **Activities.** `fill-in`, `sort` and `reflection` are behaviours of the player, like `quiz` (docs/components.md §3, with their hooks): scored, saved, recorded and announced by the player, forkable like a quiz. Templates may not hold `input`, `select` or `textarea` without a hook; the player builds those controls in the hooks.
  - **Scores.** A `fill-in` scores the share of its blanks right and a `sort` the share of its items placed right (Moodle's per-pair rule). Each is correct when all are right. A `reflection` is never scored; it counts as answered once saved.
  - **Tap first.** Placing works by choosing an item, then where it goes (WCAG 2.5.7). Drag is an extra for fine pointers; `order` also has move up and move down buttons.
  - **Saved state** (suspend data, format v1, new fields): `f` fill-in answers, `r` sort placements, `e` reflections. `t` is the dialogues'. The build's worst case counts a reflection at its `maxLength`, as a persisted string variable.
  - **SCORM 1.2 interactions:**
    - typed blanks: `fill-in`;
    - menus and words to place: `choice` per blank;
    - pairs and categories: `matching`;
    - order: `sequencing`;
    - reflection text: `fill-in`, its first 255 characters;
    - scale: `likert`.

## Words, exports and checks

- **Translation.** Every text prop is `x-translatable`. Blanks are translated with their sentence.
- **Exports.** Exports, the client view, the Document and detach render each new type as charts do: as native text. A divider renders as nothing; a gallery renders as images with captions; a sort as its items and targets in a list; a fill-in as the sentence with its answers.
- **Checks:**
  - `W_ITEMS_MANY`: tabs over 7, carousel steps over 7, markers over 8, cards over 12, a gallery carousel over 5;
  - `W_TABS_LABEL_LONG`: over 24 characters;
  - `W_QUOTE_REPEATS`: a quote whose text is also in a text block of the screen;
  - `W_ACTIVITY_NO_FEEDBACK`: a fill-in or sort without incorrect feedback;
  - `E_FILLIN_NO_BLANK`;
  - `E_SORT_TARGET`: an item pointing to a target that does not exist, pairs that do not match one to one, an order without items.

*Revisit if:* authors keep asking for a Rise variant left out (the quote carousel, the checkbox list, the button stack); a presentation's options grow beyond what one form shows (then a separate block); scroll-driven figures prove hard to set without an agent (then recipes or a figure editor).
