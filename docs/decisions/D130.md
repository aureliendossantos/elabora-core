# D130. A miniature in a browser is a live frame of the canvas page, at most eight, asked for through one call of the host (21 Nov 2026, revised 26 Nov)

The desktop shows a miniature as a capture of a hidden page (`captures.thumbnail`, an image kept on disk). A page cannot capture a page, and no browser renders a page's DOM to an image without a screen capture (`getDisplayMedia`, a permission every time; `OffscreenCanvas` draws no DOM; `<foreignObject>` redraws the page wrongly). A miniature in a browser is therefore a **frame of the canvas page** (`/canvas/index.html?mode=thumb`), at the course's reference size and scaled to its element, given a frozen render by message. The types are in `thumbs-protocol.ts`; `createThumbs` (`canvas-thumbs.ts`) is the browser's side.

Decided:
- **One call for the window** (`host.captures.show(module, scene, element)`, `CapturesShow`), a scene being a screen, a screen at a moment, a screen with a block or a charter variant. The desktop's host puts its capture in an `<img>` of the element; a browser's host calls `createThumbs`. The window has no `if (web)`.
- **At most `THUMB_LIMIT` (8) live frames**, the screen of the canvas first, then those in the view in reading order; the others keep their skeleton. A frame is 25 to 60 ms of drawing and 150 to 300 ms to load; eight cost +277 MB in Chromium, +133 MB in Firefox and +272 MB in WebKit, sixteen +441, +238 and +561 MB (a quiet Mac, a course of 62 screens). Sixteen was the limit first; it was found too much memory for an editor's tab. A view of more than eight miniatures (the Planche shows 9 to 12) draws the first eight in reading order (a line, then the left first), the others as places are freed or the author scrolls. A preview the author chooses from (« Ajouter un bloc », the Charte) comes before the miniatures under it, which the view still counts: when every place is held in the view, the last miniature in reading order gives the preview its place and takes it back once the preview goes. **Fallback if eight is still too much:** a « sheet » page, one canvas page that holds every screen as a document of its own of the same site (8 MB each, 3 times faster, measured), which needs the canvas's singletons undone (`#t-main`, the document's listeners): not now.
- **A frame outside the view is not drawn** (Chromium and WebKit never answer, Firefox in 96 s) and **moving an `<iframe>` reloads it**: a frame lives with its element, and is made only for an element in the view (`THUMB_SETTLE_MS`, 120 ms, in reading order, the editor's screen before any), destroyed with `release()`. `createThumbs` watches the view itself (an `IntersectionObserver`): **a frame whose element left the view stays while a place is free**, so a row that comes back is drawn at once, and when an element in the view needs a place and none is free, the frame farthest from the view goes. A refresh asked for out of the view is drawn when the element comes back. The window calls `show` when an element is mounted and `release()` when it goes; a window that releases rows as they leave the view is right too, it only keeps nothing.
- **A block's preview is framed as the desktop's capture is** (« Ajouter un bloc »): once the screen is drawn, the canvas fades the block's neighbours and scrolls to it (`block-preview`), and the element shows the part of the page around the block, or the block alone for the list's small box (`mini`). `ThumbShown.overflow` says whether the slide runs past the window with the block in it, from the first drawing. A miniature of the Charte is the whole window.
- **A frame kept in place is given a new render by message, never recreated**, and at most once every `THUMB_REFRESH_MS` (400 ms), `THUMB_REFRESH_PARALLEL` (2) at a time, the screen of the canvas first: a retouch in the canvas does not redraw every frame at each keystroke. A thumbnail already drawn at the render's `version` is not drawn again.
- **A miniature at rest does no work.** The page's style stops CSS transitions and animations, and the player runs no loop of its own for a still screen (the narration's clock does not play, a chart or a dialogue shows its end at once). What neither reaches is held still once the screen is drawn (`canvas-still.ts`): an animation whose rule wins over the page's or started by script is paused, an inline SVG's SMIL paused, an animated GIF drawn once and shown as that image. An animated PNG or WebP, and an SVG with SMIL in an `<img>`, still move (rarer; nothing tells or stops them at no cost).
- **A frame in the view that does not say its screen is shown in time** (a page that stopped answering, a browser short of memory) is made again after 1 s, then 4 s; then its place is freed and the window's skeleton stays until the scene is refreshed.
- **A free object is not mounted in a thumbnail**: its block is drawn as its box with the object's name. **This is where a browser's miniature differs from the desktop's capture**, which shows the object running. A video shows its poster, a sound nothing, a narration does not run.
- **The security review's rules hold**: a miniature is the canvas page itself (no new kind of document, no author's file as a page or a script), without the frames' route and without objects; one route and one port of its own for each frame (at most eight more, files only); the canvas's sandbox words, `inert`, `pointer-events: none`.
- **The engine gives the render** (`ThumbRequests`: `thumbRender`, `momentRender`, `blockPreviewRender`, `measureRender`) and declares the files it names to the relay's source.

**Measured again on a Mac (26 Nov 2026)**, with the engine's renders and the relay on the hosts' real names, on a course of 62 real screens: on a quiet machine the list of screens (8 in view) is filled in 0.30 s in Chromium, 0.41 s in Firefox 157 and 0.73 s in WebKit; after a fast scroll the view is filled within a second; sixteen live frames cost +440 MB (Chromium), +240 MB (Firefox), +560 MB (WebKit). The desktop captures one screen at a time (about 0.24 s each) and then reads its cache. Chrome's experimental `drawElementImage` (HTML drawn in a canvas, an origin trial) would let one frame give images instead (62 screens in 6.6 s, 9 KB each): a second path once it ships.

*Revisit if:* Chrome ships `drawElementImage` (HTML drawn in a canvas) as stable. One frame would then give images in Chrome, as a second path the host chooses behind `host.captures.show`; in November 2026 it is an origin trial until Chrome 160 whose form still changes, and two paths of drawing to keep are not worth it before. Eight frames are still too much memory (the sheet page). Drawing the miniatures as images on a server was measured and not kept (26 Nov 2026): a service of its own, its place of computing and authors' code on our browsers, for what live frames do well enough on the author's computer. A free object stays a box in a browser's miniature, which is accepted; a poster the object publishes is the path if that must change.
