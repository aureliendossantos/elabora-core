# D130. A miniature in a browser is a live frame of the canvas page, at most sixteen, asked for through one call of the host (21 Nov 2026)

The desktop shows a miniature as a capture of a hidden page (`captures.thumbnail`, an image kept on disk). A page cannot capture a page, and no browser renders a page's DOM to an image without a screen capture (`getDisplayMedia`, a permission every time; `OffscreenCanvas` draws no DOM; `<foreignObject>` redraws the page wrongly). A miniature in a browser is therefore a **frame of the canvas page** (`/canvas/index.html?mode=thumb`), at the course's reference size and scaled to its element, given a frozen render by message. The types are in `thumbs-protocol.ts`; `createThumbs` (`canvas-thumbs.ts`) is the browser's side.

Decided:
- **One call for the window** (`host.captures.show(module, scene, element)`, `CapturesShow`), a scene being a screen, a screen at a moment, a screen with a block or a charter variant. The desktop's host puts its capture in an `<img>` of the element; a browser's host calls `createThumbs`. The window has no `if (web)`.
- **At most `THUMB_LIMIT` (16) live frames**, nearest to the view first, the screen of the canvas first; the others keep their skeleton. Measured (39 screens, Chromium): a frame is about 20 MB resident (12 to 20) and 25 to 60 ms of drawing, 150 to 300 ms to load; 40 at once cost +860 MB and 2.4 s before the first. A page of 8 is drawn in 0.7 to 1.2 s (Chromium), 1.4 s (Firefox), up to 2.5 s (WebKit, on a loaded machine). Sixteen is 130 to 320 MB at worst. **Fallback if a real machine finds it too much:** a « sheet » page, one canvas page that holds every screen as a document of its own of the same site (8 MB each, 3 times faster, measured), which needs the canvas's singletons undone (`#t-main`, the document's listeners): not now.
- **A frame outside the view is not drawn** (Chromium and WebKit never answer, Firefox in 96 s) and **moving an `<iframe>` reloads it**: a frame lives with its element, and is made only for an element in the view (`THUMB_SETTLE_MS`, 120 ms), destroyed after a second out of it.
- **A frame kept in place is given a new render by message, never recreated**, and at most once every `THUMB_REFRESH_MS` (400 ms), `THUMB_REFRESH_PARALLEL` (2) at a time, the screen of the canvas first: a retouch in the canvas does not redraw sixteen frames at each keystroke. A thumbnail already drawn at the render's `version` is not drawn again.
- **A free object is not mounted in a thumbnail**: its block is drawn as its box with the object's name. **This is where a browser's miniature differs from the desktop's capture**, which shows the object running. A video shows its poster, a sound nothing, a narration does not run.
- **The security review's rules hold**: a miniature is the canvas page itself (no new kind of document, no author's file as a page or a script), without the frames' route and without objects; one route and one port of its own for each frame (at most 16 more, files only); the canvas's sandbox words, `inert`, `pointer-events: none`.
- **The engine gives the render** (`ThumbRequests`: `thumbRender`, `momentRender`, `blockPreviewRender`, `measureRender`) and declares the files it names to the relay's source.

*Revisit if:* a real machine's memory with sixteen frames is too much (the sheet page), or an author's object must show in a thumbnail (a poster the object publishes).
