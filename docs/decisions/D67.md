# D67. Sounds: interface sounds, sounds of the story, music and ambiences on one engine, CC0 in the core (28 Oct 2026)

The founder asked for sounds between the lines of a dialogue (the phone that rings in phishing-by-numbers), for interface sounds in dialogues (a choice, a good, fair or bad feedback, going back), default sounds that authors can switch off or replace, and later music. Research came first (28 Oct 2026: what learning research and accessibility say, Storyline and Rise, every free source and its licence, a default library, ElevenLabs' prices). The founder accepted it with four changes: music and ambiences come now, being one thing technically (a long track, looped, lowered under the voice, that learners can switch off); the core's library is CC0 only; captions of sounds are advised, never imposed; the default interface sounds are named by him. This entry decides what the research left open.

## Three layers

| | Interface sounds | Sounds of the story | Music and ambiences |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | A choice, a feedback good, fair or bad, going back or replaying, opening and closing a history or a debrief, a dialogue's successful end; a question's feedback | The phone that rings, a door, a notification: a sound that says something of the scene | An opening, a transition, a loop under a scene; an ambience of one screen |
| What starts it | Always a learner's gesture | The walk of a dialogue, a narration's timeline, an event | A screen within the range of the track, or an event (`play-sound` with `loop`) |
| Learner | On when the module has sound (below); « Sons de l'interface » in « Affichage » | Part of the content: the player's volume and mute, as the voice | « Musique et ambiances » in « Affichage » |
| Level | The course's `sounds.interface.volume` (0.5 by default) under the player's volume | The author's mix | Lowered by 20 dB at least while a voice speaks (WCAG 1.4.7) |

**When interface sounds are on.** `sounds.interface.when` is `auto` by default: on when the module has sound (a voice line whose audio is there, in a narration, a layer or a dialogue; an audio or video block; a sound of the story; music or an ambience), off otherwise, so that a silent module followed in an open space does not start clicking; `on` and `off` force it. The research found no study of click and feedback sounds in e-learning: they are for the legibility of the interface and for pleasure, not for learning, hence a default that follows what the module already does.

**The same sounds everywhere.** A question's feedback makes the sound of a dialogue's feedback (good or bad), so that a module speaks one language. The default set is the founder's choice from Kenney's Interface Sounds (CC0): `select_001` (a choice), `confirmation_001` (good), `question_001` (fair), `error_001` (bad), `scratch_004` (going back, starting over, replaying), `maximize_006` and `minimize_006` (opening and closing the history or the debrief). The successful end's jingle (Freesound 521640, « WinBanjo », CC0) needs a Freesound account to download its original: it waits for the founder, the role has no sound meanwhile. An author mutes a role (`replace: { "bad": null }`) or gives it a sound of the course (`replace: { "bad": "buzzer" }`).

## One engine in the player

- **One `AudioContext` for the page** (`@elabora/player` `sounds.ts`), made at the learner's first pointer or key press, never at load: browsers keep a context made before a gesture suspended, and an LMS frame of another origin gets the gesture in its own document. Interface sounds always follow a gesture, so they sound at once; what asked to sound before the first gesture (an ambience at the start of the first screen) starts with it. The narration and the dialogues' lines keep their own `<audio>`, the clock of their timelines (D24, D61).
- **The player's volume and mute** (the narration's controls) set the context's master gain: they apply to every sound.
- **Interface sounds** are decoded once, played from memory, at once or not at all (a click heard a quarter of a second late is worse than none).
- **Files.** The player's own interface sounds ship as `elabora/sounds/<role>.m4a` (AAC, mono, 96 kb/s, 33 KB for the seven), only in packages whose course data names them: a course without sound keeps its bytes. They are made once from the originals by `npm run sounds` (`packages/player/src/interface-sounds/sources.json`, kept by hand, with each original's SHA-256). Measured on 28 Oct 2026 in Chrome 152: `decodeAudioData` reads M4A, MP3, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis and Opus with no added silence (the encoders' priming is trimmed), so M4A, the packages' audio format since D16, serves here too.

*Not chosen:* a file per click in an `<audio>` element (a delay at each play, and iOS ignores an element's volume, which ducking needs); Web Audio for the voice (the timelines' clocks are the media elements, and they work).

*Revisit if:* an LMS's frame refuses Web Audio, or learners ask for a volume of the interface sounds of their own.
