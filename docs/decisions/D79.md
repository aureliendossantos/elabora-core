# D79. ⌘Z takes back my last step only; an inverse left out stays in force (12 Nov 2026)

⌘Z undid the journal's last step whoever made it, and an undo that kept a part of a change (its target was changed since) counted the whole change as undone: that part could never be undone again. With several people and agents on one project ([D73](D73.md)), ⌘Z would take back someone else's work, and a kept part would be lost for good.

Decided:
- **⌘Z: my last step.** The newest change of mine ([D78](D78.md)) that no undo has targeted yet, or, when it is an agent's I launched, its whole batch, as « Annuler le travail de l'IA » does (`lastStep`). Others' changes, newer or not, are passed over.
- **⇧⌘Z: my last undo, undone again,** while no change of mine came after it; others' changes in between do not count. A redo and the undo it took back are passed over, so ⇧⌘Z steps back one undo at a time (`redoStep`).
- **An inverse left out stays in force.** An undo records the inverse operations it left out (`skipped`); they are not undone (`undoState`): a change is undone only when all of its inverses are, and a batch whose undo kept something is undone in part (`BatchSummary.partly`). Undoing that batch or change again tries only what is still in force; it is not the default target of « Annuler le travail de l'IA » any more, nor of ⌘Z.
- **A step that keeps everything is still taken.** When every part of my last step was changed since, ⌘Z writes an undo that changes nothing and says what it kept, so the next ⌘Z goes further back instead of stopping there. An undo named by its number, or a batch named by its id, is refused instead (`Nothing could be undone`), as before.
- The engine picks the steps: `undo` without `seq` and `redo` (protocol), `edit.undo(dir)` and `edit.redo(dir)`; the app's ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z send them.

*Revisit if:* people ask to undo a teammate's change from the history (an explicit undo by number does it already).
