# D135. The AAC encoder in wasm is distributed, with what the LGPL asks (26 Nov 2026)

Chromium and Firefox under Linux have no AAC encoder: without one, a narration line from a WAV, a video with PCM sound or a sound to convert answers `E_MEDIA_ENCODER_MISSING` there ([D107](D107.md)). Mediabunny's `@mediabunny/aac-encoder` encodes AAC in wasm. Its JavaScript is under the MPL-2.0. Its wasm is FFmpeg's libavcodec, unmodified, under the LGPL v2.1 or later, with no GPL, version 3 or nonfree part. Revises D107, which kept it for tests.

Decided:
- **It is a dependency of `@elabora/engine`**, and a host serves it to the media Worker, which still knows it only by the address given at its opening (`aacEncoder`) and loads it only where the browser has no AAC encoder. It is never bundled into the media Worker or into what else a host serves (test `browser-base`): it stays one file of its own. `packages/engine/src/aac-encoder.ts` gives the host that file (`aacEncoderModule`, the tests' own), the facts of its provenance (`AAC_ENCODER`: FFmpeg's commit, Emscripten's version, the wasm's SHA-256, as the package's README says them, which `test/unit/aac-encoder.test.ts` checks at each upgrade) and the files its licence asks to serve beside it (`aacEncoderLicenceFiles`).
- **What the LGPL asks, read from its section 6** (the module and the app it runs in are combined in a browser, a "work that uses the Library"), and what a host does for each:
  - *A prominent notice that the Library is used and is under the LGPL, and a copy of the LGPL.* The page of the licences (in the web app, `/licences`) names it: the AAC encoder, FFmpeg's libavcodec, LGPL v2.1 or later. It links the LGPL's text (`COPYING.LGPLv2.1`) and FFmpeg's licence file, served beside the module. Where the app shows copyright notices, the encoder's are among them.
  - *The corresponding source of the Library, by one of the ways of section 6.* Way d: the module is distributed by access to a place, the app's host, so the source is offered from the same place. That source is FFmpeg at the commit the wasm was built from, as an archive, and Mediabunny's `bridge.c` and the build's commands (its README). The host takes FFmpeg's archive once from the address `AAC_ENCODER.sources.ffmpeg` gives, and serves its own copy. A link to a third party's repository is not "the same place".
  - *A way to use a modified Library.* The module is a separate file, loaded at run time by its address. Its interface is written here: an ES module exporting `registerAacEncoder()`, and its `mediabunny` is `globalThis.ElaboraMediabunny` (`CustomAudioEncoder`, `EncodedPacket`, `Logging`, `registerEncoder`). An encoder rebuilt from a modified FFmpeg by the README's commands works in its place, at that address.
  - *Terms that permit modifying the work for one's own use, and reverse engineering to debug those modifications.* The app's terms of use must not forbid this.
- Mediabunny's MPL-2.0 is met as it already is for Mediabunny itself: its licence on the page of the licences, its source at its tag (`AAC_ENCODER.sources.mediabunny`).
- An upgrade of `@mediabunny/aac-encoder` restates its provenance (`AAC_ENCODER`), and the host takes the FFmpeg archive of the new commit.

*Revisit if:* a browser reaching authors under Linux gets its own AAC encoder, or the package's wasm stops being plain LGPL FFmpeg (a GPL, version 3 or nonfree part enabled).
