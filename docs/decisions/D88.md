# D88. A first upload sends its media a few at a time, from a snapshot taken under the lock (12 Nov 2026)

Sharing a module from the app took 42 s on the test server for a module of 143 files and 60 media (5.7 MB), and 27 s for one of 100 files and 34 media (26 MB). Each medium went alone, and each request waited about 0.6 s on the network whatever its size (a 40 KiB image as long as a 4.7 MiB video): 33 s and 20 s of the two. The app's share also let the author go back to work before the upload ended (Lin 54), which the upload did not allow for: it read the files at its start but wrote the journal's last entry at its end, so an edit made meanwhile was taken for a file edit no Elabora tool made, and a medium added meanwhile was never sent.

Decided:
- **Four media at a time**, by the sender publishing already used (`cloud/blob-upload.ts`, shared by a publication and a team's media): whole up to the API's `singlePutMaxBytes`, in parts above it, read from disk as they go. The same two modules took 15 s and 11 s, their media 8 s and 5 s. The upload's own requests stay one after the other: the room's first change (about 600 KiB, 2 to 4 s), the announce and the commit (about 1 s each).
- **Progress**: `pushFolder` reports its media by bytes, then its changes one by one (`onProgress`), so the app can show the upload where the author asked for it.
- **A snapshot and its journal entry together.** `Session.cloudSnapshot()` reads the folder in the session's queue, under the project's lock, stores its binary files in the blob store and notes the journal's last entry; `pushFolder` sends that snapshot (its `source`) and writes that entry in the link. What the author, an agent or another program does during the upload is in the journal after it, and `linkCloud` (or the next opening, if the module was closed meanwhile) sends it as the changes of their authors, media included.

Weighed:
- **More than four at a time.** Four already made the media shorter than what the server's steps take; each request in flight holds up to the API's limit in memory.
- **The modal kept open until the upload ends.** Rejected: an upload of a few hundred megabytes takes minutes, and the module stays usable meanwhile.

*Revisit if:* the server announces a medium faster than a request a medium, or a module's first change outgrows the room's validation time.
