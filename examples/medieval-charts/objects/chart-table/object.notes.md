# Chart table

**Pedagogical role.** The learner lays courses (three by default, the first two to complete the table, the third optional) on a portolan chart, as a pilot would at the chart table: for each voyage they choose the wind whose rhumb lines lead from one harbour to the other (the ruler is laid along it, and every rhumb line of that wind lights up across the chart, all parallel), open the dividers to the distance against the scale of miles, then sail and see where the ship makes landfall. It serves OBJ-3 (lay a course: wind and miles). No native block can show a ruler laid on a rotated chart, nor give feedback computed from the learner's own course (which side of the harbour the ruler passes, how many miles short).

**On the page.** The chart is the figure of the lesson: it sits on the course page without a frame and appears as the learner scrolls to it (`host.onView`): the skin unrolls from its neck, the rhumb lines spread from the hidden circle, the coasts and names are inked from west to east. It never goes back once drawn. With reduced motion it appears drawn.

**Editable without code.** The voyages (a list of one to six, each from one of the 34 harbours named on the chart to another; the block's text version must follow them), how many of them complete the table, whether the first is guided, the tolerance on the distance, and the colours of the materials: the brass of the dividers and of the ruler's edge, the boxwood, the steel points, the hull. Those are material colours with literal defaults, not course tokens; the inks and the vellum of the chart are tokens of the course, which its pages use too. Every word, including the names written on the chart, is an object string: blocks may reword them and translations extract them.

**One step at a time.** The log shows the wind first; the dividers appear once a wind is chosen, Set sail once they are open. By default the first voyage is a worked example: each step waits for the one before to be right, a wrong wind says at once which way to turn the ruler, and a dashed bracket on the scale strip shows the distance to match. The strip copies the chart's scale of miles so the span of the dividers can always be read against it, even when the camera is close on a voyage.

**Tracking.** Each sailing on an open voyage is a SCORM interaction on OBJ-3 (response such as `levante-450`, correct when the wind is the nearest to the course and the distance within the tolerance). The object completes when the voyages to complete (prop `required`, two by default) have made landfall; its score is their mean of 1 at the first attempt, 0.75 at the second, 0.5 at the third, 0.25 after. Later voyages are optional practice, still recorded.

**Geometry.** objects/chart-table/chart.js: coasts in longitude and latitude, projected at the scale of 38° N and turned 9° anticlockwise like the medieval charts (magnetic north up); the hidden circle, the 32-direction rhumb network and the scale of miles (a mile of 1.25 km). The workshop figures (generators/figures.mjs) are drawn from the same module.
